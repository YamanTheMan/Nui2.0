PEER EVALUATION FORM — GROUP WORK RUBRIC — HOW TEAM MEMBERS ARE RATED

Every member of a team submits a peer evaluation form, rating themselves and each teammate. An individual's score may be adjusted based on peer evaluation feedback, and Prof Nui will talk to individuals about their team performance when needed. The form asks for the assignment or project title and the student's name and ID.

Each criterion is rated Distinguished, Proficient, Basic or Unacceptable:
- Workload. Distinguished: did a full share of the work or more, knows what needs to be done and does it, volunteers to help others. Proficient: did an equal share, works when asked. Basic: did almost as much as others. Unacceptable: did less work, does not catch up after an absence, does not ask for help.
- Organisation. Distinguished: took the initiative proposing meeting times and getting the group organised. Proficient: worked agreeably on times and places to meet. Basic: could be coaxed into meeting. Unacceptable: did not meet at agreed times and places.
- Participation in discussions. Distinguished: provided many good ideas, inspired others, communicated clearly. Proficient: participated and shared thoughts. Basic: mainly listened, sometimes made suggestions. Unacceptable: seemed bored, rarely spoke up, ideas off the mark.
- Meeting deadlines. Distinguished: completed work ahead of time. Proficient: on time. Basic: needed reminding, late but it did not affect the grade. Unacceptable: needed much reminding, lateness affected quality or grade.
- Showing up for meetings. Distinguished: punctual, sometimes early. Proficient: on time. Basic: late, but not a big problem. Unacceptable: no show or extremely late, with a feeble or no excuse.
- Providing feedback. Distinguished: habitually dignified, clear and respectful. Proficient: did not offend. Basic: some feedback, sometimes hurtful or irrelevant. Unacceptable: openly rude.
- Receiving feedback. Distinguished: graciously accepted. Proficient: accepted. Basic: reluctantly accepted. Unacceptable: refused to listen.

SYLLABUS DETAILS — PREREQUISITES — TEXTBOOK — GRADING ADJUSTMENT

Assumed prior knowledge: prior coursework in Information Systems, and comfort with case studies and applying analytical frameworks. No specialised technical prerequisites beyond standard IS program preparation.
Required textbook: Pearlson, Saunders and Galletta (2019), "Managing and Using Information Systems: A Strategic Approach", 7th edition, Wiley (ISBN 978-1-119-56115-6). It supplements class materials; students read it independently.
Grading scale: A 90–100%, B 80–89%, C 70–79%, D 60–69%, R 0–59%. These ranges guarantee the letter grade. If necessary, the instructor may adjust grades with an alternate grading policy applied to all students, explained in class.
Participation rubric note: 0% applies to a student absent from more than 4 classes, with or without reasons. Absent includes arriving after the professor.
Missing a class forfeits the marks for that class's activity. Missing a critical activity or due date needs documentary evidence certified by the Office of Health and Wellness.
References use Harvard or APA style.
